Common questions

Does Simply BP measure my blood pressure?

No. Simply BP is a diary. Measure with your home blood pressure monitor, then tap Add Reading and type in the numbers. No phone app can measure blood pressure with the camera or sensors.

Can readings from my monitor come in automatically?

Yes, if your monitor's app saves to Apple Health (Omron, Withings and many others do). In Simply BP, go to Settings › Apple Health and turn on Sync with Apple Health. New readings appear when you open the app.

I'm getting a new iPhone. How do I keep my readings?

Two ways, and you can use both:

  • Apple Health: with sync turned on, your readings are saved to Apple Health. On the new iPhone, install Simply BP, turn on Apple Health in Settings, and your history comes back.
  • Backup file: Settings › Back Up Readings saves a file wherever you choose. On the new iPhone, use Restore from Backup.

How do I share my readings with my doctor?

Tap Doctor Report on the Home or Statistics screen, choose a period, and tap Create PDF Report. You can then print it, email it, or save it. The report is part of Simply BP Pro.

I bought Pro. How do I get it back on a new phone?

Go to Settings › Restore Purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account. Pro is a one-time purchase: there's no subscription to cancel.

I have several readings a day. Which numbers does the app use?

Your history shows every reading. Averages, trends and the doctor report count each day once: readings from the same day are averaged first, so a day with five readings doesn't outweigh a day with one.

Reminders don't show up.

Check that notifications are allowed for Simply BP in the iOS Settings app › Notifications, then set your times in Simply BP › Settings › Reminders.

How do I delete my data?

Delete individual readings in History by swiping left. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your iPhone. Readings saved to Apple Health stay there until you delete them in the Health app. We don't hold a copy of your readings.

Important: Simply BP is not a medical device and doesn't give medical advice. Always talk to your doctor about your readings. If you have a very high reading with chest pain, shortness of breath, weakness or trouble speaking, call 911 or your local emergency number.
